The whole trick is what you divide by

Most people divide salary by 2,080 hours. Your employer does not own 2,080 hours of you. It owns more — and pays you on less.

Taxes come out first. Your salary is a pre-tax number; the money you live on is not. The calculator applies the effective rate you type, not a guess.

(salary − tax − commute costs) ÷ (worked hours + commute hours) × 52

Every hour counts, paid or not. The train, the traffic, the emails after dinner — the job takes them all. Only the formula on the offer letter pretends otherwise. Two offers with the same salary can differ by ten dollars an hour once the commute is counted.

Questions people ask before they argue about it

Short answers, no hedging.

Why is my real hourly wage lower than salary ÷ 40?

Because the formula ignores three things: the hours you work beyond 40, the hours you spend commuting, and the taxes taken before the money reaches you. Count all of it, and the rate drops.

Should I count my lunch break as work time?

Only if you work through it. The calculator asks for the hours your job actually owns each week, not what the contract says.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is arithmetic. Use it to compare offers, negotiate, or decide whether a raise is worth a longer commute.
