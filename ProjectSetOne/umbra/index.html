You have probably never seen the night.

You have seen a grey-orange haze where the stars should be. Almost everyone has, now. The real thing still exists, and it is closer than you think.

Step out of the haze and the sky doubles — thousands of stars instead of a handful, the Milky Way thrown across the dark like a spilled ribbon of milk. No app, no ticket, no guide required. Only distance from the lights.

Three places where the sky still falls.

Each swatch below is the colour a sky actually wears there, far from any city's glow. These are not the only ones — they are the ones to start with.

Atacama, Chile The driest desert on Earth

A sky so still and dry that major observatories moved here just to watch it. Lie back and the Milky Way is not a smear — it has texture, dust lanes, a spine.

Bannau Brycheiniog, Wales Europe's protected dark reserve

Proof you do not need a desert. Rolling moorland, old stone, and on a clear night a sky dark enough that the Andromeda galaxy shows to the naked eye.

NamibRand, Namibia Africa's dark reserve

Sand the colour of rust under a sky the colour of ink. The southern constellations wheel overhead, unfamiliar and enormous, all night long.

Watching well is a practice.

Anyone can look up. These are the small habits that turn a glance into the thing you'll remember for the rest of your life.

Give your eyes half an hour. Night vision arrives slowly — it cannot be rushed, only waited for. Stay put, keep your phone in your pocket, and let the dark accumulate. Stars you couldn't see at all will simply fade in.

Use your sideways gaze. Look slightly to the left of a faint object instead of straight at it. The eye is more sensitive to dim light off-centre — stargazers call it averted vision, and it feels like a cheat code.

Learn three things, not thirty. Find Orion, the Plough and one planet. That is enough to make every clear night for the rest of your life feel like meeting old friends.

The moon sets your night

The moon is a beautiful tyrant: bright enough to erase everything else. Plan around its phase.
