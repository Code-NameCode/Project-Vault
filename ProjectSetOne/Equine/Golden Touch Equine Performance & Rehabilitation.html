Why equine massage?

Horses are athletes. Their bodies deserve athlete-level care.

Musculoskeletal system

Massage helps release muscle tension and trigger points, supporting joints and posture.

Circulation

Hands-on work encourages blood and lymph flow, delivering nutrients and clearing metabolic waste.

Range of motion

Supple muscles and fascia allow a longer, freer stride and easier bending.

Mental relaxation

Slow, mindful touch calms the nervous system and builds trust.

Common issues massage can address

  • Muscle tightness and guarding
  • Compensatory movement patterns
  • Stiffness on one rein or in transitions
  • Recovery from intense training
  • Back soreness under saddle
  • Reduced performance or attitude changes

When to consider a session

  • Before and after competition season
  • When your horse resists tacking up or bending
  • During a return to work after rest or injury
  • As part of regular maintenance, every 4–6 weeks
  • For seniors, to stay comfortable

Massage complements, never replaces, veterinary care.

Frequently asked questions

How long is a session?

Most sessions run 45–75 minutes. Pre- and post-event sessions are shorter, around 20–30 minutes.

Is massage safe for all ages and disciplines?

Yes, for most horses. Techniques are adapted to age, fitness and discipline. Some conditions (fever, acute injury, infection) need veterinary clearance first.

What should I prepare?

A clean, quiet, level space, your horse groomed and tack removed, and any vet or training notes. Please let me know about recent injuries or changes in behavior.

How often should my horse have massage?

Competition horses often benefit every 2–4 weeks, regular riding horses every 4–6 weeks, and rehab horses as your vet recommends.

Do you work with vets and other professionals?

Always. I’m happy to coordinate with your vet, farrier, trainer, saddle fitter or chiropractor.
