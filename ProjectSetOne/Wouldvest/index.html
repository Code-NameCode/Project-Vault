The math, out in the open

Every number on this page comes from one formula and what you typed. Nothing else is added, nothing is rounded up to flatter you.

How it compounds

Each month, your habit's cost is added to a pot growing at your rate, divided over twelve months. Thirty years of $6 coffees is 360 deposits, each earning on the ones before it.

Where 10% comes from

The S&P 500 has averaged about 10% a year since 1957 with dividends reinvested — roughly 7% after inflation. Set the rate lower and the tool still works; it just tells you a quieter truth.

Straight answers

Is 10% a year realistic?

It is the S&P 500's average annual return since 1957 with dividends reinvested — about 7% after inflation. The calculator takes any rate you give it.

Where would the money actually be invested?

The math works for anything with a steady return, but most people would use a low-cost index fund inside a tax-sheltered account. Fees and taxes would trim the final figure.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is arithmetic. Talk to a fiduciary adviser before moving real money.
