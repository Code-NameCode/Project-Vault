Editorial — Our Story

We measure our work
in generations, not seasons.

In a former observatory above the Rhône, twelve people make watches the way watches were made before the word luxury lost its weight. One atelier. One bench per watchmaker. No assembly line, because an object with a heartbeat should not be born on one.

Every AURELE movement is designed, decorated and regulated in-house. Every case is milled from a single billet. We publish no annual collections; we finish a watch when it is finished, and only then do we speak about it.

A watch is the only machine we wear against the skin that must outlive our interest in it — so we build as if the wearer were someone we will never meet.

Nº 05 — Craftsmanship

Four hands,
four hundred hours.

Chapitre I

The drawing

Each watch begins as ink on paper, not a file on a server. Our draughtsman still rules his lines by hand; a computer may check the geometry, but it is not permitted to propose it.

Chapitre II

The metal

A case is turned from a single billet and rested for two weeks before finishing, so the metal can settle. Rushed metal remembers the hurry; ours is taught patience early.

Chapitre III

The fire

The dial is enamel, fused at 800 degrees and fired seven times. One firing in five cracks in the kiln. Those dials are destroyed, never sold — the kiln decides, not the calendar.

Chapitre IV

The silence

Regulation happens in a sound room built to library tolerance. For eleven days each watch is listened to, not measured — the ear catches what the machine forgives.
